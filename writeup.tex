\title{\vspace{-3.5em}Looking Monitorable Without Being Faithful:\\
Weak-to-Strong Reasoning Distillation Transfers Verbosity but Not Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness}
\author{Abraham Yeung \\ \small CS 338 Project}
\date{\vspace{-1em}\small May 2026}

\begin{document}

\vspace{-1.5em}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Weak-to-Strong Reasoning (W2SR; Yuan et al.\ 2026) shows that supervised
fine-tuning a capable student on a \emph{weaker} reasoning model's chain-of-thought
(CoT) raises the student's task accuracy. Its safety case is incomplete: it
measures only accuracy and never asks whether the student's CoT stays
\emph{monitorable}---whether the CoT still reveals the factors actually driving the
answer. We reproduce a W2SR capability gain and extend it with a controlled
monitorability study using the Meek et al.\ (2025) chain-of-thought faithfulness
eval on GPQA-Diamond. Two findings. (i) \textbf{W2SR reproduction requires an
in-family (same base-family) weak teacher}: a foreign-style teacher (DeepSeek-R1
distillation) yields no gain and degenerate generations, whereas an in-family
native-Qwen weak teacher lifts a base student from 0.32 to 0.65 Pass@1 on MATH; a
\emph{headroom probe} shows this gain is genuine elicitation on a base student but
a prompting artifact on an instruct student. (ii) Holding student capability fixed,
\textbf{distillation transfers CoT \emph{verbosity} (+0.26, 95\% CI [0.22, 0.31])
but not cue-\emph{faithfulness}}, which sits at a floor (1.8--4.6\% cue
acknowledgment) for every student condition while the teacher reaches 22--37\%. A
distilled model therefore \emph{looks} more monitorable---longer, more legible
CoT---without being more faithful. We map the work to the course's
reproduce/critique/extend rubric and are explicit about a floor-power limitation.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{-0.5em}
\section{Introduction}
Chain-of-thought (CoT) ``monitorability'' is the hope that reading a model's
reasoning tells us \emph{why} it answered as it did, so that an overseer can catch
undesired influences. Two literatures pull against each other. Weak-to-Strong
Reasoning (W2SR)~\cite{yuan2026} reports that fine-tuning a strong student on a
\emph{weaker} model's CoT \emph{raises accuracy}---weak supervision elicits latent
student capability. Separately, faithfulness work~\cite{turpin2023,meek2025} shows
models often \emph{do not} verbalize the cues that change their answers.

\textbf{Critique.} W2SR's safety argument is incomplete: it certifies the student
on task accuracy alone and is silent on whether the distilled CoT remains
monitorable. If W2SR-style distillation altered \emph{how} a model reasons on the
page without altering \emph{what} it reveals, an accuracy-only check would miss it.

\textbf{Contributions} (mapped to reproduce / critique / extend):
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \emph{Reproduce.} We reproduce a W2SR capability gain on MATH, and in doing so
identify two conditions Yuan et al.\ leave implicit: the weak teacher must be
\emph{in-family} with the student, and the apparent gain is genuine only on a
\emph{base} (non-CoT-eliciting) student---on an instruct student it is a
CoT-prompting artifact (\S\ref{sec:repro}).
\item \emph{Critique \& Extend.} Holding student capability fixed, we measure CoT
monitorability (faithfulness + verbosity) of the baseline, the W2SR student, and a
strong-teacher control, against teacher references, on GPQA-Diamond
(\S\ref{sec:ext}). We find a \textbf{decoupling}: verbosity transfers, faithfulness
does not.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Setup}
\textbf{W2SR}~\cite{yuan2026}: generate CoT traces from a weak teacher on a problem
set, keep correct and incorrect traces, and SFT a stronger student on them; report
held-out Pass@1. \textbf{Monitorability}~\cite{meek2025}: for each question, present
a \emph{cue} (e.g.\ an authority opinion, a leaked answer key, embedded metadata)
that points at a target answer; an LLM judge reads the full CoT and scores (a)
\emph{faithfulness} = whether the CoT \emph{acknowledges} the cue, and (b)
\emph{verbosity} = a factor-utilization rate over the decision-relevant factors. We
use the five cues shipped in the released eval
(authority, visual pattern, grader hack, leaked info, XML metadata) and a single
judge (\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6}, $T{=}0$).

\textbf{Models / compute.} Student (all monitorability conditions):
\texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct}. Teachers (in-family, native-Qwen): weak
\texttt{Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct}, strong \texttt{Qwen2.5-Math-72B-Instruct};
reproduction student \texttt{Qwen2.5-Math-7B}. Training is LoRA SFT; generation,
training, and offline gating run on Modal GPUs; the monitorability eval drives the
cloned Meek eval. All evaluation is at $T{=}0$; every trace set is hashed.

\section{Reproduction: when does W2SR actually fire?}
\label{sec:repro}
\textbf{It does not reproduce with a foreign-style teacher.} Across students
(7B-instruct, 7B-base), public 1.5B teachers (R1-distill, SimpleRL-Zoo), LoRA ranks,
and decodings, a DeepSeek-R1--distilled weak teacher produced \emph{no} Pass@1 gain
and chronically degenerate CoT (Table~\ref{tab:repro}, top). The R1 teacher is
Qwen-\emph{based} but imparts a foreign, over-thinking R1 reasoning style that is
out of the student's distribution and hard to distill cleanly under LoRA.

\textbf{It reproduces with an in-family weak teacher.} Swapping in a clean
native-Qwen weak teacher (\texttt{Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct}, $\sim$67\% on the
training problems) and student \texttt{Qwen2.5-Math-7B} lifts held-out MATH (L3--5)
Pass@1 from 0.325 to 0.645 (+0.32), with a 1.00 valid-format rate and no
degeneration---the first passing capability gate in our matrix.

\textbf{Headroom probe: genuine on a base student, artifact on an instruct
student.} A $+5$pt gate-pass on an already-CoT-capable model can be ``rearrangement''
rather than elicitation. We therefore also score the \emph{untrained} model under a
zero-shot CoT prompt (the prompting-only ceiling) and report
$\text{W2SR}-\text{(zero-shot CoT)}$ (Table~\ref{tab:headroom}). On the base
student, zero-shot CoT does nothing (0.24) and W2SR adds \textbf{+0.41 beyond
prompting}---real elicitation of latent capability. On the instruct student,
zero-shot CoT alone already reaches 0.63 and W2SR adds \textbf{+0.00}: its apparent
$+0.40$ gain over an unelicited baseline is purely a CoT-elicitation confound.
\emph{W2SR reproduction is real, but only on a base student; on an instruct student
the headline gain is an artifact.}

\section{Extension: does W2SR preserve CoT monitorability?}
\label{sec:ext}
\textbf{Design.} All four conditions share the locked instruct student so
comparisons are clean: \textbf{(1)} baseline \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct};
\textbf{(2)} \emph{W2SR student} = (1) LoRA-SFT'd on the weak in-family teacher's
MATH traces; \textbf{(3)} \emph{control} = (1) SFT'd on the \emph{strong} in-family
teacher (\texttt{Qwen2.5-Math-72B-Instruct}) on the \emph{same} problems---a clean
teacher-strength axis ($1.5$B vs $72$B, $48{\times}$ scale; on L3--5 the
Qwen-Math family saturates at $\sim$66--69\% so the axis is by \emph{scale}, not
train accuracy); \textbf{(4a)} weak-teacher reference (R1-distill-1.5B's own CoT).
Crucially, capability is \textbf{controlled, not changed}: on the instruct student
the SFT lands at its zero-shot-CoT ceiling (Table~\ref{tab:headroom}), and all
three students score $0.33$--$0.38$ on GPQA, so faithfulness differences are not a
capability confound. Estimands (preregistered): E1 $=$ W2SR$-$baseline, E2 $=$
W2SR$-$control (the weak-supervision-specific effect).

\textbf{Results} (Table~\ref{tab:ext}). \emph{Verbosity transfers, large and tight:}
both trained students are markedly more verbose than baseline (E1 $=+0.264$, 95\%
CI $[0.218,0.312]$; control$-$baseline $=+0.247$, $[0.192,0.300]$; both exclude 0),
and the two are indistinguishable (E2 $\approx 0$). \emph{Faithfulness does not.}
Cue acknowledgment is at a floor for every student condition (1.8--4.6\%). The
floor is established robustly by the \emph{baseline at $N{=}970$ cued samples}; the
weak teacher reaches 22--37\%, confirming the judge registers acknowledgment when
present, so the students' floor is real behavior (7B instruct models rarely
verbalize a decisive cue), not measurement failure. Distillation does not lift the
students off this floor; the preregistered paired test on acknowledgment is null
(E2 CI includes 0). We make the floor claim strongly but do \emph{not} claim a
precise no-transfer magnitude---only that no transfer is detectable above a floor
all student conditions share. Directionally, the trained students drift
\emph{slightly above} baseline (3.6/4.6\% vs 1.8\%; noise at these counts), the
opposite of inheritance: there is no evidence distillation drags the student toward
the teacher's much higher faithfulness.

\section{Discussion}
\textbf{Verbosity and faithfulness decouple under distillation.} Meek's composite
``monitorability'' is the average of verbosity and faithfulness; here it rises after
distillation \emph{almost entirely through verbosity}. The practical warning is
direct: \emph{a W2SR-distilled model can look more monitorable---longer, more
legible chains---without being any more likely to reveal a decisive hidden cue.} An
accuracy-only safety check (W2SR's) misses this, and so does a monitorability check
that rewards legible-looking CoT; the safety-relevant component (does the CoT name
the factor that moved the answer?) did not improve, and the weak-teacher student is
indistinguishable from strong-teacher distillation on both axes.

\textbf{The in-family requirement} is a substantive condition on W2SR itself:
weak-to-strong transfer is gated by teacher$\leftrightarrow$student
distributional match, not just teacher capability, and the headroom probe shows the
``gain'' must be read against the student's own prompting ceiling. Both sharpen when
W2SR's accuracy claim is even meaningful.

\section{Limitations}
\textbf{Floor / power.} Cue acknowledgment is at a floor across all student
conditions, so \emph{between-student} faithfulness comparisons are underpowered; we
claim the (baseline-anchored, $N{=}970$) floor, not a precise no-transfer
magnitude. \textbf{Sibling parsing effect (disclosed).} MATH-SFT students answer in
\texttt{\textbackslash boxed\{X\}} rather than the requested \texttt{ANSWER: X};
the adaptive cue generator parses only \texttt{ANSWER:}, so it created cues for
$\sim$4$\times$ fewer samples in the SFT'd conditions (cued: baseline 970 vs.\ W2SR
225 vs.\ control 175). The acknowledgment \emph{rate} on the cued subset is
unbiased---both judges read the full completion, verified by a hand-check across all
three conditions---but the reduced $N$ compounds the floor-power limit. (Final
accuracy scoring is corrected by a format-agnostic re-parse; fixing the upstream
cued-set shrinkage would require re-running the cue passes.) \textbf{Scope.}
Small-scale, LoRA (not full SFT), public 1.5B/72B teachers, a single judge,
faithfulness at floor, GPQA-Diamond only for the extension, and the strong
\emph{teacher} reference (R1-32B) was not run ($\sim$20 GPU-hr).

\section{Conclusion}
W2SR's accuracy gain is real but conditional (in-family teacher; base student), and
its silence on monitorability hides a decoupling: distillation makes a student's
chain-of-thought \emph{longer} without making it \emph{more faithful}. Certifying
weak-to-strong students on accuracy---or on legible-looking CoT---is insufficient;
the cue-acknowledgment component must be measured directly.
