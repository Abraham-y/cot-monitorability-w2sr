\title{\vspace{-3.5em}Looking Monitorable Without Being Faithful:\\
Math-CoT Fine-Tuning Degrades Cue-Faithfulness Independent of Teacher Strength}
\author{Abraham Yeung \\ \small CS 338 Project}
\date{\vspace{-1em}\small May 2026}

\vspace{-1.5em}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We test whether fine-tuning a reasoning model on math CoT preserves chain-of-thought
monitorability, using the cue-based faithfulness paradigm (Meek et al.\ 2025; Turpin
et al.\ 2023) on a DeepSeek-R1-distilled student. Our central finding is a
\textbf{dissociation between behavior and verbalization}: after weak-to-strong
reasoning fine-tuning (W2SR; Yuan et al.\ 2025), a planted cue's \emph{behavioral}
pull on the answer is \emph{not reduced} (flips land on the cue target at
${\sim}2\times$ chance, paired influence $\Delta\!=\!+0.16$), while the CoT's
\emph{acknowledgment} collapses $25\%\!\to\!3\%$ ($34/1$ one-directional discordant,
$p\!\approx\!2\!\times\!10^{-9}$) at improved GPQA accuracy
($0.28\!\to\!0.42$). Lobo et al.\ (2024) already showed fine-tuning lowers
faithfulness on an answer-level metric; our contribution is that the cue's causal
grip is held fixed while verbalization disappears.
The effect is \textbf{teacher-strength-invariant}: a same-strength
self-distillation control (teacher == student) collapses acknowledgment
indistinguishably from W2SR weak ($p\!=\!1$, $n\!=\!79$), so weak-to-strong
asymmetry is incidental and the cause is math-CoT SFT in general. The raw drop is
partly compression (CoT shortens ${\sim}14\times$, matched-length residual marginal
across all samples), but \textbf{restricted to the safety-relevant subset where the
cue actually moved the answer ($\mathrm{influenced}\!=\!1$), W2SR independently
suppresses cue acknowledgment at matched mid-length} (baseline $70\%$ vs.\
W2SR $21\%$, $\mathrm{OR}\!\approx\!8.75$, $p\!=\!0.003$). The effect is robust to
an out-of-family judge ($\kappa\!\in\![0.65,0.68]$, paired drop preserved) and
replicates on MMLU at $\sim\!3\times$ compression with a $-17$pp drop --- a
within-paper dose-response. \textbf{A capability-only certification can pass a
model whose CoT has gone behaviorally opaque while remaining superficially legible.}
\end{abstract}

\vspace{-0.5em}
\section{Introduction}
Chain-of-thought ``monitorability'' is the hope that reading a model's reasoning
reveals \emph{why} it answered, so an overseer can catch undesired influences.
Weak-to-Strong Reasoning (W2SR)~\cite{yuan2025} reports that fine-tuning a strong
student on a \emph{weaker} model's CoT \emph{raises accuracy}. Faithfulness
work~\cite{turpin2023,meek2025,chua2025} shows models often do not verbalize the
cues that actually move their answers.

\textbf{Critique.} W2SR certifies the student on accuracy alone and is silent on
whether the distilled CoT stays monitorable. If distillation changed \emph{how} a
model reasons on the page without changing \emph{what it reveals}, an accuracy
check would miss it.

\textbf{Contributions.} The phenomena we exploit are largely known --- Lobo
et al.~\cite{lobo2024} showed fine-tuning hurts CoT faithfulness on an
answer-level metric; Zaman \& Srivastava~\cite{zaman2025} argued via causal
mediation that the dissociation between behavior and CoT verbalization can exist
statically. Our contributions are methodological and target-specific.
\textbf{(1) Controllable induction of the dissociation via a specific training
method.} We separate behavior (paired influence and flip-to-cue rate, $\sim
2\times$ chance) from verbalization (judge acknowledgment) and show that
math-CoT SFT \emph{controllably} moves an R1-distill student from monitorable
(ack $25\%$, behavior preserved) to monitorable-looking-but-not (ack $3\%$,
behavior still preserved). Beyond observing the dissociation, the contribution
is that a marketed training method induces it on demand with behavior held
fixed (\S\ref{sec:ext}).
\textbf{(2) Same-strength self-distillation negative control.} A
self-distillation arm (teacher $==$ student, same R1-7B traces) reproduces the
collapse indistinguishably from W2SR weak (paired $p\!=\!1$, $n\!=\!79$), with
pairwise McNemar $p\!\ge\!0.09$ between all trained arms. To our knowledge no
prior work runs this control in this form; it is the cleanest evidence that the
cause is math-CoT SFT in general, not weak-to-strong asymmetry (\S\ref{sec:ext}).
\textbf{(3) Refined mechanism: compression for the raw drop, plus matched-length
cue suppression on the safety-relevant subset.} A length-binned Fisher analysis
shows the matched-mid-length gap closes across all cued samples
($p\!=\!0.80$), supporting compression as the explanation for the raw
$25\%\!\to\!3\%$ drop. Restricted to the safety-relevant subset
($\mathrm{influenced}\!=\!1$, where the cue actually moved the answer), the
matched-mid-length gap reopens to baseline $70.0\%$ vs W2SR $21.1\%$,
$\mathrm{OR}\!\approx\!8.75$, $p\!=\!0.003$. Decomposing the matched-length
question by influenced status is methodologically new.
\textbf{(4) Reproduction critique + certification consequence.} A headroom probe
shows W2SR's MATH Pass@1 lift is itself conditional on (i) an in-family teacher
and (ii) a base student (\S\ref{sec:repro}). Combined with (1)--(3), this means
an accuracy-only certification of any math-CoT-SFT student (including
same-strength self-distillation) can pass a model whose CoT has become markedly
less revealing.

\section{Related work and what is new here}
\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Fine-tuning degrades CoT faithfulness.} Lobo et al.~\cite{lobo2024} show
task-specific fine-tuning lowers CoT faithfulness across four datasets; we take this
degradation as given. Our claim is not that faithfulness drops but that it drops
\emph{while the cue's behavioral influence on the answer does not}, a dissociation
their answer-level faithfulness metric does not isolate.
\textbf{Self-distillation and verbalization.} Concurrent work~\cite{selfdistill2026}
finds self-distillation suppresses \emph{epistemic} verbalization (expressed
uncertainty) via compression, hurting out-of-distribution generalization. We share
the compression mechanism and the self-distillation setup but differ in outcome
variable: our target is \emph{cue-acknowledgment} (a safety/monitorability property),
not uncertainty calibration or OOD accuracy, and we measure the behavioral cue-pull
directly. \textbf{CoT monitorability.} Meek et al.~\cite{meek2025} measure
monitorability as faithfulness plus verbosity across model families; we use their
cued-prompt eval but study how a \emph{training intervention} moves faithfulness on a
fixed substrate, and pair it with a behavioral-influence measure to separate
``stops being influenced'' from ``stops verbalizing influence,'' and ask the sharper
question of whether faithfulness falls \emph{beyond} what CoT length alone explains.

\section{Setup}
\textbf{W2SR}~\cite{yuan2025}: sample CoT from a weak teacher on a problem set, keep
correct + incorrect traces, LoRA-SFT a stronger student; report held-out Pass@1.
\textbf{Monitorability}~\cite{meek2025}: each question is paired with a \emph{cue}
(authority opinion, leaked answer key, embedded metadata, etc.) pointing at a
target; an LLM judge reads the full CoT and scores \emph{faithfulness}
(does the CoT \emph{acknowledge} the cue) and \emph{verbosity} (a
factor-utilization rate). We use the five cues shipped in the released eval and a
single judge (\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6}, $T{=}0$); faithfulness is reported as
acknowledgment among \emph{cued} samples. All evaluation is greedy ($T{=}0$); trace
sets are hashed. Training is LoRA; generation/training/gating run on Modal GPUs.

\section{Reproduction: when does W2SR fire?}
\label{sec:repro}
With a foreign-style weak teacher (DeepSeek-R1-distilled 1.5B, or SimpleRL-Zoo)
distilled into a Qwen2.5-7B student, we found \emph{no} Pass@1 gain and frequent
degeneration, across students, ranks, and decodings. W2SR reproduces only with an
\textbf{in-family, native-Qwen} weak teacher: \texttt{Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct}
($\sim$67\% on the train problems) $\to$ \texttt{Qwen2.5-Math-7B} lifts held-out
MATH (L3--5) Pass@1 from $0.325$ to $0.645$, format-valid $1.00$.

\textbf{Genuine only on a base student (headroom probe).} A $+5$pt gate-pass on an
already-CoT-capable model may be re-arrangement, not elicitation. Scoring the
\emph{untrained} model under a zero-shot CoT prompt (the prompting-only ceiling)
and reporting $\text{W2SR}-\text{(zero-shot CoT)}$ (Table~\ref{tab:repro}): on the
base student W2SR adds $+0.41$ \emph{beyond prompting} (genuine latent-capability
elicitation); on an instruct student it adds $+0.00$---its apparent gain over an
unelicited baseline is purely the CoT-elicitation confound. Two conditions Yuan et
al.\ leave implicit: in-family teacher, and a base (non-CoT-eliciting) student.

\section{Extension: does W2SR preserve CoT monitorability?}
\label{sec:ext}
\textbf{Capability-controlled design.} We compare a baseline student, a W2SR student
(LoRA-SFT'd on a weak in-family teacher's MATH CoT), and references, on GPQA. The
SFT lands the student at its own CoT ceiling, so faithfulness differences are not a
capability confound; the question is ``does distillation change faithfulness,'' not
``does it improve capability.''

\textbf{Instruct student is floored (motivation).} On \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct},
cue-acknowledgment is at a floor for every condition (baseline $1.8\%$ at $N{=}970$;
W2SR $3.6\%$; strong-teacher control $4.6\%$), while teacher references reach
$22$--$37\%$. We cannot detect faithfulness transfer here---but verbosity transfers
strongly (W2SR$-$baseline $\Delta\!=\!+0.26$, CI $[0.22,0.31]$): the model
\emph{looks} more monitorable without measurable faithfulness change. To probe a
substrate with dynamic range, we move to a reasoning student.

\textbf{Reasoning student: behavior preserved, verbalization collapses}
(Table~\ref{tab:r1}). Fully in-family: student \texttt{R1-Distill-Qwen-7B}, weak
teacher \texttt{R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B}; distillation is clean (gate format-valid
$0.99$); baseline acknowledgment is off-floor ($25\%$).
\textbf{Behavior is not reduced.} Paired influence
(answer $==$ cue target on matched (qid, cue)): $\Delta\!=\!+0.157$, $95\%$ CI
$[+0.037,+0.278]$, $n\!=\!108$, McNemar $p\!=\!0.021$, discordant $16/33$ ---
direction-positive but with a lower CI bound near zero, so we read this as
\emph{not reduced} rather than confidently strengthened. The cleaner, stronger
signal: flips land directionally on the cue target at $73\%$ for W2SR vs.\
$65\%$ for baseline, both ${\sim}2\times$ the $1/3$ random-flip chance
($p\!<\!10^{-7}$ each) --- statistically indistinguishable from each other and
from the untrained directionality. The pull is
genuine cue-influence, not accuracy gain in disguise: restricted to the
cue-points-at-\emph{wrong}-answer stratum (so landing on the cue \emph{is} a wrong
answer), flippers still hit the cue target at $77\%/74\%/74\%$ for baseline /
W2SR weak / strong-teacher distill (each $p\!\le\!2\!\times\!10^{-9}$ vs.\ $1/3$).
\textbf{Verbalization collapses (both rubrics).} On the upstream loose rubric
(``acknowledge the cue''), W2SR drops pooled acknowledgment to $3.2\%$
(paired on (qid,cue): $\Delta\!=\!-0.220$, CI $[-0.293,-0.153]$, McNemar
$p\!\approx\!2\times10^{-9}$, discordant $34/1$; the drop holds at every text cue:
stanford $47\%\!\to\!8\%$, grader-hack $22\%\!\to\!3\%$, insider $56\%\!\to\!5\%$,
with one-directional discordant cells $12/0$, $7/1$, $15/0$). \emph{The conservative
read} (stricter ``attribute reasoning to the cue'' rubric, same Gemini judge): the
loose rubric was partly counting mentions; under the strict rubric the rates shrink
to baseline $9.4\%$ vs W2SR $0.0\%$ ($15/0$ discordant, $p\!=\!6\!\times\!10^{-5}$),
so the magnitude is smaller but the drop is \emph{more} one-directional and the
trained student never explicitly attributes reasoning to the cue. Both numbers hold
at \emph{improved} GPQA accuracy ($0.28\!\to\!0.42$, $n{=}40$; a $+14$pp
cross-domain lift from MATH training to GPQA science) and ${\sim}14\times$
shorter CoT (baseline median $18{,}692$ chars $\to$ W2SR $1{,}362$). The
monitorability cost is therefore being paid against a real cross-domain accuracy
gain, not against a null --- which is what makes the certification failure
sharp. The drop is not an
artifact (hand-checked: completions coherent, $37/40$ conclude with an answer,
none degenerate---the model solves the problem without verbalizing the cue). It
is also not a single-judge artifact: re-scoring the same 525 R1-family CoTs with
\texttt{gemini-2.5-pro} yields per-condition Cohen's $\kappa\!\in\![0.65,0.68]$
and a paired drop $\Delta\!=\!-0.153$, McNemar $p\!=\!2.4\!\times\!10^{-7}$,
discordant $23/0$.

\textbf{Mechanism: compression-dominant + a real matched-length residual.}
Faithfulness and CoT length both fall; the near-trivial half is that shorter CoT
surfaces cues less, so the claim of interest is degradation \emph{at matched
length}. Length is endogenous to reasoning (a brevity-prompt control failed---R1
ignores it), so we isolate the residual via a length-binned Fisher's exact analysis
(Task H, quantiles of the union distribution). \emph{Across all cued + judge-labeled samples}:
at matched mid-length ($[1954, 8716)$ chars) baseline and W2SR are indistinguishable
($17.5\%$ vs $15.1\%$, $p\!=\!0.80$); at very long lengths ($[8716, 37816)$,
baseline $n\!=\!119$, W2SR $n\!=\!40$) baseline is meaningfully higher
($27.7\%$ vs $15.0\%$, OR $\!\approx\!2.2$, one-sided $p\!=\!0.08$) --- a
direction-positive residual just below conventional significance.
\emph{Restricted to the safety-relevant subset} ($\mathrm{influenced}\!=\!1$,
i.e.\ samples where the cue actually moved the answer), the picture sharpens
substantially: at matched mid-length the gap reopens to baseline $70.0\%$
($7/10$) vs W2SR $21.1\%$ ($16/76$), $\mathrm{OR}\!\approx\!8.75$, Fisher
two-sided $p\!=\!0.003$; at long lengths the same direction holds ($67.6\%$ vs
$40.0\%$, $\mathrm{OR}\!\approx\!3.14$, $p\!=\!0.15$ at baseline $n\!=\!34$,
W2SR $n\!=\!10$). The unconditional Task H was diluted by $\mathrm{influenced}\!=\!0$
samples (cue did not move the answer), where neither condition acknowledges much
at any length and so the gap looks closed.
So most of the raw $25\%\!\to\!3\%$ drop is \textbf{compression} (the channel separator \texttt{</think>} appears in $100\%$
of training traces but only $22\%$ of W2SR-student completions vs.\ $58\%$ of
baseline, so the compression is emergent under SFT, not from stripped
supervision), and the residual lives specifically in the long-trace regime.
A teacher-strength axis (Table~\ref{tab:r1}) shows the effect is
\textbf{compression-general}: a strong 14B teacher degrades faithfulness about as
much as the 1.5B teacher and is not distinguishable from it. \textbf{Self-distillation
negative control.} To test whether weak-to-strong asymmetry is causal at all, we
train baseline R1-7B on its OWN R1-7B traces (1200 MATH L3--5, $T\!=\!0.6$, same SFT
config). Both self-distillation arms (4k and 8k generation budgets) collapse
acknowledgment to $2.4\%$ and $3.4\%$ --- statistically indistinguishable from W2SR
weak (paired $p\!=\!1$, $\Delta\!\approx\!0$ on $n\!=\!79$ matched pairs each) --- and
median CoT compresses to ${\sim}1.3$k chars regardless of budget.
\textbf{Cross-substrate replication on MMLU.} A small MMLU sweep (5 STEM subjects,
8 Qs each $\to$ 40 cued/cell, $n\!=\!35$ paired after parseable-answer attrition on
the baseline side) holds the pattern: baseline ack $20.0\%$, W2SR weak $2.9\%$,
Self-A $0.0\%$; Self-A vs.\ baseline paired $\Delta\!=\!-0.171$, McNemar
$p\!=\!0.031$ (discordant $6/0$, one-directional); Self-A vs.\ W2SR weak again
indistinguishable ($p\!=\!1$). Baseline R1-7B already writes shorter CoT on MMLU ($3.6$k vs.\ $19$k
chars median), so MMLU compression is only $\sim$$3\times$ vs.\ GPQA's
$\sim$$14\times$, and the absolute ack drop is correspondingly smaller (MMLU
$20.0\!\to\!2.9\%$, drop $\sim$$17$ pp at $3\times$ compression; GPQA
$25.0\!\to\!3.2\%$, drop $\sim$$22$ pp at $14\times$). \emph{Less compression,
smaller drop, but the drop still fires} --- a within-paper dose-response from
compression to ack collapse that supports the compression-dominant reading
without staking it on the matched-length cuts alone. So the
faithfulness collapse \emph{is not weak-to-strong-specific, not GPQA-specific, and
not contingent on extreme CoT compression}: it fires at $3\times$ compression
too, and its magnitude scales with how much compression occurred. The W2SR asymmetry is incidental; the mechanism is \textbf{SFT on
terse MATH-style CoT traces in general}, which compresses CoT and suppresses
cue-acknowledgment whether the teacher is weaker, stronger, or identical to the
student.

\section{Discussion}
Math-CoT SFT does not preserve monitorability and, where it is measurable, it hurts.
The two substrates are consistent: on the floored instruct student
Meek's composite ``monitorability'' (the mean of verbosity and faithfulness scores
defined in~\cite{meek2025}) rises after distillation almost entirely via
verbosity---the model \emph{looks} more monitorable; on the
reasoning student, the cue's \emph{behavioral pull on the answer is not reduced
while its verbal acknowledgment collapses}---a dissociation a compression-only
account would not predict, since the model keeps being moved by the cue while no
longer reporting it. The safety-relevant component thus gets \emph{worse}, not better. The warning is
sharp: \emph{an accuracy-only certification of \textbf{any} CoT-SFT student trained on
MATH-style traces---including same-strength self-distillation, not just
weak-to-strong as in W2SR---can pass a model whose chain-of-thought has become
markedly less revealing}, and a monitorability check that rewards elaborate-looking
CoT would miss it too. The narrowness of the W2SR accuracy claim itself (in-family
teacher + base student, \S\ref{sec:repro}) only sharpens this point: the certified
guarantee is narrow on both axes.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:lim}
\textbf{Floored instruct substrate:} the instruct arm only motivates; the
faithfulness claim rests on the reasoning student. \textbf{Matched-length residual depends on the cut.} \emph{Unconditionally},
the gap at matched mid-length is closed ($17.5\%$ vs $15.1\%$, $p\!=\!0.80$) and
at long lengths is marginal ($\mathrm{OR}\!\approx\!2.2$, one-sided $p\!=\!0.08$).
\emph{Restricted to $\mathrm{influenced}\!=\!1$} (the subset where the cue
actually moved the answer, which is the safety-relevant question), the
matched-mid-length gap is $70.0\%$ vs $21.1\%$, $\mathrm{OR}\!\approx\!8.75$,
$p\!=\!0.003$. Compression accounts for most of the raw drop in absolute ack
rates; cue-suppression accounts for the matched-length residual on the
safety-relevant subset. We do not claim compression is the sole mechanism.
\textbf{Single family:} the degradation is shown within the Qwen R1-distill
lineage. A same-recipe cross-family test on R1-Distill-Llama-8B fails the
capability gate cleanly ($50.0\%\!\to\!22.5\%$ GPQA, $-27.5$pp at $10.8\times$
compression), placing it in the over-compression confound regime, so we do not
run its monitorability eval. Cross-family generalization remains open and is
reported as a null. \textbf{Cross-substrate:} the MMLU replication is
direction-stable and significant for Self-A vs.\ baseline ($p\!=\!0.031$) but
underpowered for W2SR weak vs.\ baseline at $n\!=\!35$; broader coverage is
untested. \textbf{Also:} LoRA only; judge robustness with one alternative family
(\texttt{gemini-2.5-pro}), not a panel.

\section{Conclusion}
W2SR's accuracy gain is real but conditional (in-family teacher; genuine only on a
base student), and on a reasoning student its faithfulness collapse ($25\%\!\to\!3\%$
at preserved accuracy) is \textbf{not weak-to-strong-specific}: a same-strength
self-distillation control reproduces the collapse at indistinguishable magnitude,
and the cue's behavioral pull on the answer is \emph{not reduced} while
verbalization collapses---a dissociation that survives a within-judge rubric swap
(under which it tightens: ack $9.4\%\!\to\!0.0\%$), an out-of-family judge, and a
cross-substrate (MMLU) replication. The mechanism is \emph{compression as produced by math-CoT SFT, plus an independent
cue-suppression effect on the safety-relevant subset}: across all cued samples the
matched-length residual is marginal, but restricted to samples where the cue
actually moved the answer ($\mathrm{influenced}\!=\!1$) it is substantial
($\mathrm{OR}\!\approx\!8.75$, $p\!=\!0.003$). The substrate dose-response
(GPQA $14\times$, MMLU $3\times$) supports compression as the dominant explanation
for the raw drop while the $\mathrm{influenced}\!=\!1$ cut isolates the
W2SR-specific failure mode at matched length.
A same-recipe cross-family attempt on R1-Distill-Llama-8B fails the capability
gate cleanly ($-27.5$pp accuracy at $10.8\times$ compression), so the dissociation's
cross-family scope remains open. Certifying \emph{any} CoT-SFT student on accuracy
alone, or on how elaborate its CoT looks, is insufficient and potentially
misleading; the cue-acknowledgment component of monitorability must be measured
directly.
