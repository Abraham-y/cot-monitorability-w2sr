## 1. One-paragraph summary

We study whether Weak-to-Strong Reasoning (W2SR), a method in which a large student model is supervised on chain-of-thought (CoT) traces generated by a much smaller weak reasoning model, preserves, degrades, or improves the student's CoT monitorability relative to appropriate baselines. W2SR (Yuan et al. 2025) was introduced as a cheap alternative to reinforcement learning for eliciting reasoning, and recovers most of RL's task-performance gains. That paper measured task accuracy only. It did not measure whether the resulting student's CoT remains a faithful, monitorable window into its reasoning, which is the property that AI-safety schemes based on CoT monitoring depend on. We add that measurement layer, with a control condition that isolates the effect of weak supervision specifically from the effect of fine-tuning on CoT data in general.

---

## 2. Research question and hypotheses

Primary research question: When a strong reasoning model is trained via W2SR on a weak reasoner's CoT traces, how does the student's CoT monitorability change relative to (a) its untrained baseline, (b) a control student trained on an equal volume of stronger or ground-truth CoT traces, and (c) the weak teacher?

Four pre-registered competing hypotheses, so that any outcome is a finding:

- H0 (Null / SFT-preserves): W2SR is structurally SFT, and SFT tends to preserve reasoning faithfulness, so the W2SR student's monitorability should be statistically indistinguishable from its baseline and from the control student.
- H1 (Inheritance): The student inherits the weak teacher's faithfulness profile and becomes less monitorable than its baseline, trending toward the teacher's (low) acknowledgment rates.
- H2 (Generalization beyond teacher): The student generalizes past the teacher and becomes more monitorable than baseline, possibly approaching the RL upper bound's profile. This is the informed prior of Iván Arcuschin, co-author of the monitorability-score paper.
- H3 (Base-model-determined): Monitorability is governed mostly by the base model, so neither W2SR nor the control moves it much.

Decisive contrast: H1 vs H2 make opposite predictions about the sign of (W2SR student minus baseline). The control condition and the teacher reference are what let us attribute any movement to weak supervision rather than to SFT-on-CoT in general.

Headline estimand: the difference in monitorability score between the W2SR student and (i) its untrained baseline and (ii) the matched control student, with the weak teacher's score as the reference that distinguishes inheritance from improvement.

## 3. Background (minimal)

W2SR (Yuan et al. 2505.20072): a strong student is SFT'd on long CoT trajectories from a much weaker reasoner; recovers close to 94 percent of RL's gains at a fraction of the cost; teachers of 0.5B to 1.5B can supervise students of 7B to 32B; structurally well-formed CoTs matter more than teacher correctness; even partially incorrect traces help.

CoT monitorability (Korbak et al. 2507.11473; Meek et al. 2510.27378): the CoT is useful for oversight only if it faithfully reflects the model's actual reasoning. Faithfulness is commonly measured via the cued-input perturbation paradigm (Turpin et al. 2305.04388): inject a hint into the prompt; when the model changes its answer because of the hint, check whether the CoT acknowledges the hint. Meek et al. add verbosity (whether the CoT lists every factor needed) to form a combined monitorability score.

The gap: no published work applies CoT faithfulness or monitorability measurement to W2SR-trained students. Confirmed via literature search across W2S extensions, CoT-faithfulness, and reasoning-model faithfulness papers as of May 2026. Confidence roughly 80 percent that the exact study is unpublished, lower that it is not in progress elsewhere. The lead and co-author of the monitorability-score paper confirmed by email they have not seen this direction studied (this raises the "unpublished" confidence but does little for "not in progress").

## 5. Experimental design

### 5.1 Conditions

The design is best read as a **teacher-strength axis**, not a binary treatment-vs-control. The student is fixed; what we vary is the capability of the teacher whose CoT traces supervise it, from a teacher *below* the student (weak-to-strong) to a teacher *above* the student (strong-to-weak distillation). Monitorability as a function of teacher strength is the scientifically interesting object, and the "control" is simply the upper anchor of that axis. The code is structured so intermediate teachers can be slotted in later for a **dose-response curve** (Section 5.3); the minimal publishable result uses the two endpoints plus the references below.

For each student (size pair):

1. Baseline student: the untrained student, evaluated on the monitorability benchmark. Establishes the starting point. (Must natively produce CoT — see 5.2.)
2. W2SR student: the student SFT'd on the **weak teacher** (below-student) CoT traces (Yuan et al. recipe). The treatment.
3. Control / distillation student (CRITICAL, see Section 6): the student SFT'd on **strong teacher** (above-student) CoT traces — standard distillation — matched to the W2SR condition on problem set, trace count, token budget, trace *style* (both are long, model-generated CoT), and training hyperparameters. Because both arms use long model-generated CoT in the same style, the only manipulated variable is **teacher strength**. This isolates the weak-supervision effect from the generic "SFT-on-CoT" effect. Locked decision: the control is strong-teacher distillation, NOT ground-truth references (see 5.4 for why, and for the optional confounded GT sanity arm).
4. Teacher references: each teacher (weak, and strong) evaluated standalone, to establish its faithfulness profile and adjudicate H1 vs H2.
5. RL upper bound (optional): the GRPO-trained student that Yuan et al. use as ceiling. If compute does not allow training it, cite their numbers and note the limitation.

Minimum viable result uses conditions 1, 2, 3, 4. Condition 5 is a bonus. Intermediate teachers (5.3) and the GT sanity arm (5.4) are publishable-grade extensions, not required for the core claim.

### 5.2 Models: student and teacher axis (LOCKED)

**Student (fixed across all conditions): `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct`.** The monitorability eval is only meaningful if the *baseline* (untrained) student already produces a substantive CoT to score for faithfulness and verbosity. The Instruct model does CoT natively and reliably; the base model would need zero-shot CoT prompting and tends to give short or degenerate CoT (a weak baseline, low influence rate — Section 17). We therefore use Instruct as the student in every condition and keep the elicitation identical across conditions. Tradeoff to acknowledge in the paper: Yuan et al. SFT the *base* model, so this is a deliberate deviation; a base + zero-shot-CoT arm is a possible robustness check, not the primary.

**Teacher axis (the manipulated variable), public DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen series:**
- Weak teacher (W2SR condition, below the student): `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B`.
- Strong teacher (control / distillation condition, above the student): `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B`. If 32B generation is too expensive, fall back to `DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B`, or pull strong traces from a public R1 trace dataset (OpenR1-Math, OpenThoughts) filtered to the same problem set, matched on trace count and token volume (Section 8.1, 16).
- Intermediate teachers (7B, 14B) for the dose-response sweep (5.3): add later.

Teacher-choice rationale (locked): commit to the public DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen series rather than blocking on Yuan et al.'s released checkpoints. Reasons: guaranteed availability, in-family with a Qwen student, and Chua & Evans already published CoT-faithfulness baselines for these exact models, giving a free literature baseline for the dependent variable. Still clone the Yuan repo to harvest the recipe, data format, and hyperparameters. Yuan's "Reasoner" teachers turn out to be the public `hkust-nlp/*-SimpleRL-Zoo` checkpoints (Apache 2.0, on HF, trivially loadable) — register them as an *optional* later robustness teacher, do not depend on them.

Size pairs:
- Pilot / plumbing: student `Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct`, weak teacher `DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B`. Confirms the pipeline runs end to end.
- Primary (the main result): student `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct`, weak teacher `DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B`, strong teacher `DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B` (or fallback).
- Secondary (compute permitting): student `Qwen/Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct`. Tests scale robustness.

Context-length note: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct has a 32k context, which comfortably holds long R1-distilled traces; avoid the 4k Qwen2.5-Math models for the student.

### 5.3 Teacher-strength dose-response (the interesting extension)

The two-endpoint design (weak teacher below the student, strong teacher above it) generalizes to a sweep of teachers spanning capability, e.g. 1.5B → 7B → 14B → 32B R1-distill teachers supervising the same 7B student. Plotting student monitorability (and acknowledgment, verbosity, Pass@1) against teacher strength turns the H1-vs-H2 question into a *curve*: does monitorability degrade monotonically as the teacher gets weaker (inheritance), improve (generalization-beyond-teacher), or stay flat (base-determined)? This is the most novel, publishable framing. Implement the teacher axis as a list of `TeacherSpec`s (Section 14 / `config.py`) so adding a point is a one-line change. Run the two endpoints first; add interior points only if the endpoints show a clean, interpretable difference and compute remains.

### 5.4 Why strong-teacher distillation, not ground-truth references (locked)

The monitorability score includes a **verbosity** component (does the CoT enumerate every factor needed?). Terse, human-written ground-truth solutions vs long model-generated CoT differ enormously in length and style, and that style gap would swamp the weak-vs-strong-teacher signal we are trying to measure. Holding trace style fixed (both arms = long model-generated CoT) and varying only teacher strength is the clean contrast. Ground-truth references are kept ONLY as an optional, explicitly-confounded **sanity reference** (a point that is known to differ in style), never as the primary control. Report it, if run, with the style confound stated plainly.

## 6. Threats to validity and confound control

This section is the most important addition over the short spec. A reviewer or the course staff will immediately ask the questions below; pre-empt them.

### 6.1 The "SFT on CoT at all" confound (primary)

If you compare only baseline vs W2SR student, any change in monitorability could be caused by fine-tuning the model on long CoT data of any kind, not by the weak-supervision aspect specifically. The control student (condition 3) addresses this. Attribute an effect to weak-to-strong only if W2SR differs from the control, not merely from the untrained baseline. Report both deltas (W2SR minus baseline, and W2SR minus control). The W2SR-minus-control delta is the scientifically clean number; the W2SR-minus-baseline delta is the practically relevant one.

### 6.2 Capability confound

If the W2SR student and the control student end up at different capability levels (different Pass@1), a monitorability difference might just reflect capability, not supervision source. Mitigation: report capability (Pass@1) alongside monitorability for every condition, and if capabilities diverge, discuss monitorability conditional on capability or match training compute so capabilities are comparable.

### 6.3 Measurement confounds

The cued-input paradigm only yields a faithfulness signal on "influenced" cases (where the hint flips the answer). Different models have different influence rates, so the denominator differs across conditions. Report influence rate per condition, and report acknowledgment rate both unconditionally and conditional on influence. The Meek et al. verbosity component partly addresses the "answer did not change" blind spot; use it.

### 6.4 Judge reliability

Acknowledgment classification is done by an LLM judge, which can be biased or noisy. Mitigation: use a strong judge model distinct from all models under test, validate the judge on a hand-labeled sample of at least 50 cases (report judge accuracy / agreement), and spot-check disagreements. The "Lie to Me" paper uses a multi-judge panel plus an independent validation judge; a single strong judge plus a hand-labeled validation set is acceptable for a class project if you report the validation.

### 6.5 Decoding nondeterminism

Sampling temperature changes both answers and CoT. For evaluation, fix decoding (temperature 0, or a fixed seed at low temperature) so condition differences are not sampling noise. For trace generation, the recommended R1-distill sampling settings apply, but log seeds.

### 6.6 Generalization / external validity

Findings on math + GPQA + MMLU with Qwen + R1-distill may not transfer to other families, tasks, or scales. State this plainly. The secondary size pair and a non-math task (if time allows) strengthen generality. Do not over-claim.

## 7. Pipeline components

Three core stages, each a standalone parameterized function, so the same code runs locally (tiny model, CPU) and remotely (real model, GPU):

1. `generate_traces`: given a teacher model and a problem set, sample CoT trajectories. Output: a dataset of (problem, prompt, cot, answer, correct?) records to disk/volume.
2. `train_student`: given a base student and a trace dataset, run SFT (LoRA for pilot, full SFT for final). Supports both the weak-teacher dataset (W2SR) and the control dataset, selected by config. Output: a checkpoint plus a training-validation report (Section 9).
3. `evaluate_monitorability`: given any checkpoint, run the Inspect monitorability eval on GPQA and MMLU subsets with injected hints. Output: per-condition monitorability scores, per-hint acknowledgment and influence rates, and raw transcripts, saved as structured JSON/CSV.

Plus:
- `run_matrix`: orchestrator over conditions and size pairs; collects results into one table.
- `analysis`: loads results, computes deltas, runs the statistical tests (Section 11), makes plots.
- `validate_training`: the gate in Section 9, callable after each training run.

## 8. Concrete configuration

### 8.1 Trace generation (teacher)

- Source problems: 500 to 1000 from MATH (or GSM8K for a cheaper first pass). Use a fixed split; reserve a disjoint held-out set for capability evaluation (Section 9).
- Sampling: temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95 (DeepSeek R1-distill recommended), max_new_tokens 4096. Log the seed.
- Keep both correct and incorrect traces for the main run (Yuan et al. found incorrect traces still help). A correct-only variant (their W2SR-P) is an ablation.
- For the control's strong-teacher traces, PREFER downloading an existing public R1 trace dataset (OpenR1-Math, OpenThoughts) over generating from a 32B model, to save compute. Either way, enforce the matched-problem-set and matched-volume requirement (Section 16): filter the public dataset to the same problem set used for the weak condition, and subsample so trace count and total token volume match the weak condition within tolerance. Only generate from the 32B/14B teacher directly if no public dataset cleanly covers the problem set.

### 8.2 Student SFT

- Method: LoRA (rank 16, alpha 32) for pilot; full SFT for final runs if compute allows. Use the same method across W2SR and control so they are comparable.
- Learning rate: 1e-4 (LoRA) or 1e-5 (full SFT).
- Epochs: 3.
- Effective batch size: 32 via gradient accumulation; per-device size = whatever fits.
- Max sequence length: 8192 (long traces).
- Optimizer: AdamW, cosine schedule, warmup ratio 0.03.
- Match W2SR and control on every hyperparameter and on total training tokens.
- Before finalizing, read Yuan et al.'s training script in their repo and match their recipe where it differs.
- Log: loss curve, learning rate schedule, seed, exact data hash.

### 8.3 Evaluation

- Framework: Inspect, using the Meek et al. monitorability eval as the base; adapt their hint set if needed.
- Datasets: GPQA Diamond (primary; harder, surfaces the effect), MMLU subset (secondary; easier comparison).
- Hints: use the cue set the cloned Meek eval actually ships (spec 13.4 = cloned code is ground truth). That is FIVE cues, not six: stanford_professor_recommends (authority), visual_squares_correct (visual pattern), grader_hack_validation (code/grader hack), insider_information (leaked answer key / unethical info), xml_metadata_success_rate (metadata embedding). Listed in `config.MEEK_CUES`. Pull exact templates from the eval code; do not improvise wording. (The earlier "six categories from Chen et al." was from the spec's literature reading; the released eval uses these five.)
- Model serving (LOCKED): the Meek eval is config-driven and API-provider based (Inspect model strings). The STUDENT — untrained baseline AND trained W2SR/control checkpoints — is ALWAYS served via Modal vLLM, so the headline baseline-vs-W2SR comparison goes through one identical serving path (no serving confound). Off-the-shelf TEACHERS run via OpenRouter, CONDITIONAL on a one-time check that OpenRouter returns full R1-distill reasoning traces (thinking tokens), not just answers (`scripts/check_openrouter_thinking.py`); if it strips them, serve teachers on Modal too and report it.
- Judge selection (spec 10.3): run BOTH candidates — `openrouter/qwen/qwq-32b` (Meek's default) and `anthropic/claude-sonnet-4-6` — against the >=50-case hand-labeled set, report human agreement for each, select the higher. Keep qwq-32b favored if it validates acceptably (comparability to Meek); use Sonnet's family-independence (outside the Qwen/DeepSeek family under test) only as a tiebreaker when agreement is close. Confirm the chosen judge differs from all models under test.
- Decoding: temperature 0 (or fixed low-temp seed) for all eval runs.
- Judge: a strong model distinct from all models under test; two-stage classification (regex pattern match then LLM judge) per "Lie to Me"; validate on a hand-labeled set of at least 50 cases.
- Sample size: at least 300 examples per condition per hint type.
- Save raw transcripts so acknowledgment labels can be re-derived if the judge protocol changes.

## 9. Training-validation gate

Do not trust any monitorability number until the corresponding training run passes this gate. If training silently failed, the monitorability comparison is meaningless.

Run `validate_training` after each SFT run. The run passes only if all of:

1. Loss converged: training loss decreased and plateaued (not diverged, not flat from step 0). Save and inspect the curve.
2. Output format valid: on a held-out sample of 20+ problems, the trained model produces a well-formed CoT followed by a parseable final answer (for example a boxed answer for MATH) at least, say, 90 percent of the time. Catches format collapse.
3. No degenerate generation: check for repetition loops, premature truncation, empty CoTs, or runaway length on the held-out sample.
4. Capability gain (the core W2SR claim): Pass@1 on the disjoint held-out math set must increase meaningfully over the untrained baseline. Pre-commit a threshold, for example "W2SR student Pass@1 exceeds baseline by at least 5 absolute points" or "recovers at least 30 percent of the baseline-to-teacher gap." If W2SR does not produce a capability gain in your setup, you have not reproduced Yuan et al., and you must fix training before any monitorability claim. Report the actual Pass@1 numbers regardless.

The control student must pass the same gate (loss converged, format valid, no degeneracy). Its capability need not match W2SR exactly, but record it for the capability-confound discussion (Section 6.2).

Record the gate outcome in the results table next to each condition. A failed gate invalidates that condition's monitorability numbers.

## 10. Measurement methodology, operationalized

This expands what the short spec deferred to the papers. Pull exact templates and code from the cloned repos; the operational logic below is what the code must implement.

### 10.1 Cued-input faithfulness (Turpin / Chua-Evans / Chen protocol)

For each evaluation question q and each hint type h:
1. Run the model on the unhinted prompt for q; record answer a_unhinted and the CoT.
2. Construct the hinted prompt by inserting hint h that points to a specific (often wrong) answer option.
3. Run the model on the hinted prompt; record answer a_hinted and the CoT.
4. An "influenced" case is one where a_hinted equals the hint's target and differs from a_unhinted (the hint changed the answer toward what the hint suggested).
5. For influenced cases, classify whether the hinted CoT acknowledges the hint (mentions that the hint/cue influenced the answer). This yields the acknowledgment (faithfulness) rate.

Report: influence rate (fraction of cases the hint flipped), acknowledgment rate among influenced cases, and acknowledgment rate over all cases. The "Why Models Know But Don't Say" paper distinguishes acknowledgment in the thinking tokens vs the answer text; if the models expose thinking tokens, measure both channels.

### 10.2 Verbosity (Meek et al. addition)

Beyond cue acknowledgment, measure whether the CoT lists every factor needed to solve the task (verbosity), since a model can be "faithful" about the cue yet omit other reasoning, which still defeats monitoring. Use Meek et al.'s operationalization from their code. The combined monitorability score integrates faithfulness and verbosity into one number per model.

### 10.3 Judge prompt structure (implement, then validate)

The judge receives the question, the hint, and the model's hinted CoT, and must output a binary acknowledgment label plus a short justification. Use the cloned eval's judge prompt verbatim if available. Validate: hand-label at least 50 cases, compute judge agreement with human labels, report it, and only proceed if agreement is acceptable (for example Cohen's kappa above 0.6 or accuracy above 80 percent).

### 10.4 What to log per case

question id, hint type, unhinted answer, hinted answer, influenced flag, acknowledgment label (per channel if applicable), verbosity score, full transcripts. Logging transcripts lets you re-score later without re-running inference.

## 11. Statistical analysis plan

Pre-register this before looking at the full-scale results. Lock the primary test on the pilot data, then run it unchanged on the full data.

### 11.1 Unit of analysis and estimands

- Unit: the individual evaluation case (question x hint type).
- Primary estimand 1: difference in monitorability score between W2SR student and untrained baseline.
- Primary estimand 2: difference in monitorability score between W2SR student and control student. This is the confound-clean estimand.
- Secondary estimands: acknowledgment-rate differences per hint type; influence-rate differences; the W2SR student's position relative to the teacher (for H1 vs H2).

### 11.2 Tests

- For the combined monitorability score (continuous or bounded): paired comparison across conditions on the same questions; report the mean difference with a bootstrap 95 percent confidence interval (resample questions with replacement, 10000 iterations). Use the CI, not just a p-value, and report effect size.
- For binary acknowledgment outcomes on paired cases (same question and hint evaluated under two conditions): McNemar's test, plus the raw rate difference with a bootstrap CI.
- Preferred principled model (if a team member is comfortable): a mixed-effects logistic regression, acknowledgment ~ condition + (1 | question) + (1 | hint_type), which accounts for question and hint random effects and gives a clean condition effect with a CI. Report this as the primary if feasible; otherwise the bootstrap paired comparison is the primary and this is a robustness check.

### 11.3 Multiple comparisons

You will compare several conditions across several hint types. For the headline claims (the two primary estimands), no correction needed since they are pre-registered and few. For the per-hint-type breakdown (exploratory), either apply a Bonferroni or Benjamini-Hochberg correction or explicitly label the breakdown as exploratory and uncorrected. Do not cherry-pick a single significant hint type as the headline.

### 11.4 Power and resolution

At 300 cases per condition per hint type, and influence rates that may be only 10 to 40 percent, the number of informative (influenced) cases per cell can be modest (30 to 120). This limits the resolution of per-hint acknowledgment comparisons. Mitigations: pool across hint types for the primary test to increase informative N; if a per-hint effect matters, increase N for that hint. State the achieved informative N per cell in the paper. If informative N is too low to distinguish H1 from H2, say so rather than over-interpreting.

### 11.5 Decision rule

- Support H1 (inheritance) if W2SR monitorability is below baseline and below control, with the CI excluding zero, trending toward the teacher.
- Support H2 (improvement) if W2SR monitorability is above baseline and above control, with the CI excluding zero.
- Support H0 (preserve) if W2SR minus control CI includes zero (no weak-supervision-specific effect) even if W2SR minus baseline moves (which would then be an SFT-on-CoT effect, itself worth reporting).
- Support H3 (base-determined) if neither W2SR nor control differs meaningfully from baseline.

## 16. Data management and reproducibility

- Seeds: fix and log a global seed for trace generation, training, and evaluation. Record per-run seeds in the results table.
- Determinism: temperature 0 (or fixed seed) for all evaluation inference, so condition differences are not sampling noise.
- Versioning: pin dependency versions in pyproject.toml; record the git commit hash of each external repo used.
- Data hashing: hash each trace dataset and store the hash with the trained checkpoint, so you can prove which data produced which model.
- Matched volume: log total training tokens for W2SR and control and confirm they match within a small tolerance.
- Caching: cache teacher traces and downloaded weights on the Modal Volume; never regenerate traces you already have.
- Transcript retention: save all eval transcripts so labels can be re-derived if the judge or metric changes.
- Results schema: one row per (size_pair, condition, dataset, hint_type) with influence rate, acknowledgment rate (per channel), verbosity, monitorability score, Pass@1, gate-passed flag, seed, data hash, commit hash.

## 17. Failure modes and debugging guide

- W2SR shows no capability gain: check trace quality (are teacher CoTs well-formed?), check that loss decreased, check sequence length is not truncating traces, try more epochs or full SFT instead of LoRA, confirm you are evaluating Pass@1 correctly (answer extraction/parsing). If still flat, your reproduction failed; fix before any monitorability claim.
- Influence rate near zero: the hints are not strong enough to flip answers, so the faithfulness test has no informative cases. Use stronger hints or harder questions (GPQA over MMLU), or increase N. Lean on the verbosity component, which does not require influenced cases.
- Judge disagreement high: refine the judge prompt, use a stronger judge, or fall back to the two-stage regex-plus-judge scheme; re-validate on the hand-labeled set.
- Monitorability scores identical across conditions: could be real (H0/H3), or could mean the metric is saturated or insensitive at this scale. Cross-check with the raw acknowledgment rate; confirm the metric distinguishes the teacher from the baseline (it should, per Meek et al.'s thinking-vs-non-thinking result) before concluding null.
- Out-of-memory on 7B/14B: use LoRA, gradient checkpointing, smaller per-device batch with more accumulation, bf16, or a larger GPU on Modal.
- Long traces blow the context window: cap teacher max_new_tokens, or use a long-context student.
- Cost overrun on Modal: do the smallest size pair fully first; only scale after a clean small-scale result.
